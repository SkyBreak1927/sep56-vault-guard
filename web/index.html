Four of the security risks SEP-56 calls out

SEP-56 lists rounding, overflow, and access control among its security concerns, and describes a donation attack on share price. When the donation attack succeeds, the victim can lose their entire deposit.

Donation / inflation attack

FAIL on the reference vault

An attacker first deposits 1 stroop, then donates 100,000,000 stroops straight to the vault, bypassing deposit(). A depositor who then puts in 5,000,000 stroops receives 0 shares, and their deposit is absorbed into the vault.

Raising decimals_offset to 6 or burning dead shares at construction did not make the check pass. Protection has to be sized to the donation worth defending against, for example with a deployer-seeded initial deposit.

Rounding direction

Share math must round in the vault's favor: deposit() rounds shares down and mint() rounds assets up, as SEP-56 specifies.

The check caught a rounding bug deliberately injected into a test vault.

Extreme inputs

A deposit of i128::MAX must fail cleanly, leaving total_assets at 0 instead of producing a wrong result.

Access control

An operator without approval cannot withdraw an owner's shares.

With an allowance, the operator can withdraw within it, the allowance drops by exactly the amount spent, and a withdrawal beyond it is rejected.
